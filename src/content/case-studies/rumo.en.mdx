  A 3D landing page tied to the scroll that went **from zero to live without me writing a line of
  code**. AI agents wrote all of it; I directed, reviewed and built the harness that tells them how
  to work on this project.
</Tldr>

## Context and problem

I wanted to prove one thing in practice: can you ship an ambitious front end, with a 3D scene,
scroll-driven animation and a phone version, **only by directing AI agents**? I picked a theme that
pushes the browser hard: **Rumo**, a fictional travel agency where the whole site is a film.

The camera passes through an airplane window, drops through the clouds to the destination, the cards
fly into a dotted globe, the globe unrolls into a boarding pass and the page returns to the cabin, at
night, with the form.

## My role

I didn't touch the code. My job was the one of whoever leads the build:

- **Direction**: what each scene shows, the brand identity, what goes in and what stays out.
- **Review**: checking every delivery in the browser, on desktop and phone, and asking for fixes.
- **Harness engineering**: writing the environment the agents work in, so they get it right
  without me repeating the rules on every request.

## Constraints

- **Zero hand-written lines**: every change, even a single colour, went through the agents.
- **No framework**: plain Vite, so the 3D and the animation stay light.
- **60 fps** with a live Three.js scene behind the text.
- **Accessible**: visible focus, touch on phones and respect for reduced-motion preferences.

## The harness

The heart of the project is a guide (`CLAUDE.md`) that every agent reads before changing anything.
It turns personal taste into rules that can be checked:

| Part of the guide | What it says                                                                                                |
| ----------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Identity**      | The palette as tokens, a single orange accent, the heading and body fonts, where the logo and favicon live. |
| **Rules**         | Colours only through tokens, curves only from one easing file, headings in at most 2 lines, a single pin.   |
| **Motion**        | Scroll drives the camera and the image; time drives the text. No scene just appears: each hands over.       |
| **Where to edit** | A table that goes from "I want to change the price" to the exact file.                                      |
| **How to check**  | The screen sizes to review, scene by scene, plus a clean console and a passing build.                       |

With it, a short request like "make the accent orange" becomes a consistent change across the CSS,
the wall shader and the globe's routes, without me pointing at each file.

## Implementation highlights

- **Airplane windows modeled in code**, with shader-driven blinds and the view built from layered
  photos.
- **A dotted globe with routes** that draws itself and then unrolls into a flat map, printed on the
  boarding pass.
- **Line-masked text** that comes in on time, whatever the scroll speed.
- **Reduced motion**: no smooth scrolling, hard cuts and opacity only.

## Result and impact

The site has been **live on GitHub Pages** since November 2026: about 4,200 lines of JavaScript and CSS across 38 files, none typed by me.

What I take into practice: **with agents, the bottleneck stops being typing and becomes specifying
and reviewing**. The better the harness, the less rework and the more consistent the result.

## What I'd do differently and next steps

The structure and animations started from a landing page template, and the photos are still the
demo's. The next step is generating my own photos for each destination (the recipes are already in
the repository) and wiring the form to a real submission; today it only confirms on screen.

The site's brand, numbers, prices and contacts are fictional.
